# What Makes a Game Fun (feel, pacing, risk/reward, novelty, social): top 20 videos

How these were picked: yt-dlp search across 6 queries for this topic, about 25 results each, Shorts removed. Videos were ranked by reach (log of views) and relevance (how many queries returned them, and how high), then hand-checked for topic fit. View counts are from Nov 2026.

How much to trust each takeaway: **17/20** are well-documented talks or essays, summarised from what they're known to cover. The other 3 are **from title + description**: YouTube blocked transcript downloads from the cloud session, so read those as hypotheses until you've watched the video or run `research/tools/fetch_transcripts.py` on your own computer.

## Recurring patterns across the 20

- **Juice the most-repeated action most.** Squash/stretch, particles, sound layers, small screen shake, number pop-ups and hit-pause make simple actions satisfying.
- **Feedback before content.** Several talks show the same mechanic going from dull to fun purely through feedback polish.
- **Risk/reward and interesting decisions.** Let players choose bigger risk for bigger payoff; avoid single obvious best choices.
- **Pacing in waves.** Alternate tension and release; introduce new mechanics regularly; stop for big moments.
- **Randomness with care.** Use input randomness for variety; make output randomness feel fair (pity, visible odds).
- **Intrinsic > extrinsic.** Mastery, curiosity, expression and social connection sustain fun; rewards should point at them, not replace them.
- **Social moments.** Cooperation, showing off and friendly competition create memorable, shareable moments.
